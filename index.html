About

I'm a computer engineering junior at Texas A&M University, graduating May 2027. I work at the boundary where hardware meets software: the layer where a design decision shows up as real cycles, real watts, real latency.

That range is deliberate. I've written Verilog for a custom accelerator and benchmarked it cycle-by-cycle in simulation; I've also shipped AI features inside enterprise software where the constraint isn't the model, it's the system around it. The common thread is wanting to know why the number is the number: measuring before claiming, and being honest about what a benchmark does and doesn't say. The same instinct runs the rest of my life. Ask anyone who's seen how I organize a pickup soccer bracket.
